Sineless layout concepts

Two ways to arrange the same interface, revised after the first round. Each holds every control from the brief, shown at its real size in the modes that matter. They are grey on purpose: colour, type details and the orange accent come in step 3.

v1 had three concepts; the Player bar was dropped in v2. This is v6. Frames are drawn at their real pixel size and scaled to fit. Traces use sample signals shaped like the plugin's mock tracks; lanes differ only by brightness for now. Brief and audit: Sineless UI audit.

A · Toolbar

One bar on top, the screen below

Like a Mac app's unified toolbar. Every control that applies in the current mode is in one row above the screen, always. A status bar under the screen holds the readouts, the cursor buttons and the state.

  • Nothing folds away. There is no ⋯ menu. The window can't get narrower than the widest toolbar, so every control always fits.
  • Span, Scale, Views and Trigger use icons, at every width. Their labels are gone from the toolbar, so nothing changes shape as the window narrows; the Trigger popover still has its title. The icons are placeholders in grey: Span is a width between two stops, Scale a height between two stops, Views two traces, Trigger an edge crossing a level. Step 3 draws them properly.
  • Only the wordmark ever hides. The widest case is Single · Seconds with the trigger mode on Single, where Re-arm shows. Measured on that case:
    • 1080 px and wider: mark and wordmark. This is the default, 1080 × 580.
    • 1020 to 1079 px: the mark alone. The minimum is 1020 × 400.
  • Group is a dropdown ("Group A ▾"), and Compare loses its "beta" tag. Help can say it's in beta instead.
  • Overlay | Stack is in the toolbar, in every mode: it also arranges the lanes in Multi and Compare.
  • New status bar order. Left: Time, Level, Hover, then A4. Centre: what the readouts measure, then Peak, RMS and Freq. Right: the state, then Help. The buttons never change width and the state area has a fixed width, so the readouts stay put. They are centred between the two sides, a few pixels off the window's centre.
  • Readouts hold still. Each label sits right next to its value and unit, and each readout has a fixed width, so a longer value never pushes the next one.
  • The readout tag says Left, Right, Mid or Side in Single, and This track in Multi and Compare. I didn't use Sum in Compare: the readouts measure this track's Mid there, not the sum (DESIGN.md §1.2.1). Showing Sum would need the engine to measure the sum instead, which is a behaviour change.

More states than fit

The state area has a fixed width, sized for three chips and Help, so the readouts never move. When more states are active, the ones that don't fit fold into one "+n" chip, and resting the pointer on it lists them. Chips keep the order of the state table in DESIGN.md §1.5, so Frozen and the pan offset stay in view. The last frame shows five states with +3 open.

B · Inspector

The screen, with a panel at its side

Like the inspector in Logic or Final Cut. The top bar keeps the mode, the group and Freeze. Settings sit in a panel on the right, in labelled sections. In Multi and Compare the panel lists the tracks with their data. A status bar under the screen holds the readouts and the state.

  • One control per line, each with a label, as in the trigger section: Time base, Span and Scale; Channels and Layout; the six trigger settings; Time, Level and Hover as switches; Concert pitch and Help.
  • The panel is 225 px wide (was 252). That needs one more change: in the panel the trigger mode is a dropdown, like Span. As the four-way Auto | Normal | Free | Single switch with a label beside it, it alone would make the panel 316 px wide.
  • The cost is height. With every line separate, Single · Seconds needs a window 710 px tall, so that is B's default and minimum height with the panel open. Below that the panel would have to scroll.

My recommendation

Still A. With nothing folding away it is simple to describe: every control that applies is in the toolbar, always. B's one-control-per-line panel is easy to read, but it makes the plugin tall, and a tall plugin window competes with the DAW's own mixer and arrange views.
