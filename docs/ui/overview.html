Sineless · Plugin UI redesign · Overview

Sineless plugin UI

The final design for the Sineless interface: one toolbar on top, a screen inset in a graphite case, colour only in the traces, and orange for Frozen alone.

This page brings together the three design steps and what we chose in each: the layout (concept A, Toolbar), the visual direction (Graphite, accent on Frozen) and the colour tokens. Frames are drawn at their real pixel size and scaled to fit. The token values live in sineless-tokens.json (source of truth) and sineless-tokens.css; the plugin's own copy is ui/src/tokens.css. Overview

The three display modes at the default size, 1080 × 580.

Layout · concept A

One bar on top, the screen below

Like a Mac app's unified toolbar. Every control that applies in the current mode is in one row above the screen, always. A status bar under the screen holds the cursor buttons, the readouts and the state.

  • Toolbar, left to right: the mark and "Sineless"; Single | Multi | Compare; Group (Multi and Compare); Beats | Sec, Span and Scale; Views (Single) and Overlay | Stack; Trigger and Re-arm (Seconds); then Freeze and Auto-freeze at the right.
  • Nothing folds away. There is no ⋯ menu. Controls that don't apply are hidden, not dimmed: Views in Multi and Compare, Trigger in Beats, Group in Single, and Re-arm unless the trigger mode is Single.
  • Span, Scale, Views and Trigger use icons, at every width. Their names appear in the open popovers, so nothing changes shape as the window narrows.
  • Group, Views and Trigger open popovers. The trigger popover holds mode, source (in Single), slope, level, hysteresis and hold-off; the field shows the current mode.
  • Status bar. Left: Time, Level and Hover cursors, then A4. Centre: what the readouts measure, then Peak, RMS and Freq. Right: the state chips, then Help.
  • Readouts hold still. The buttons never change width, the state area has a fixed width and each readout has a fixed width, so a longer value never pushes anything.
  • The readout tag says Left, Right, Mid or Side in Single, and This track in Multi and Compare (the readouts measure this track's Mid there, DESIGN.md §1.2.1).
  • More states than fit fold, from the last, into one "+n" chip that lists them on hover. Invalid samples, a problem in the audio, comes first, so it always shows; the others keep the order of the state table in DESIGN.md §1.5.

Visual direction · Graphite

A display in a case

The chrome is dark graphite and the screen is an inset black display with rounded corners, like the glass on a hardware instrument. Controls are filled grey without outlines; the selected option is white.

  • Type. Geist for every control, in sentence case at 12.5 px; Geist Mono for every number, with even-width figures. The old wide-tracked capitals are gone.
  • Identity. The cashew mark and "Sineless" in Geist Semibold open the toolbar.
  • Selection is white on graphite, so what's on is unmistakable without any colour.
  • Cursors, the playhead, the hover line and the zoom band are white or grey. Cursors carry the letters A and B.

Field icons

Drawn on a 12 px grid with a 1.3 px stroke and round ends, to sit beside Geist Mono values.

Colour and tokens

Five rules

  • The chrome is greys. Graphite case, darker controls, white for what's selected. No colour means "active" or "on".
  • Colour is data. A colour on screen is always a channel or a track.
  • Orange means Frozen, on the Frozen button and the Frozen chip, and nothing else.
  • Yellow means a problem: invalid samples, or tracks that cancel when summed.
  • Everything else is white or grey. The old green, blue and pink state colours are retired; the words in each chip carry the meaning.

Chrome and screen

Channel colours

Left blue, Right pink, Mid white, Side lime. None is orange or amber, so the accent stays unique. In Multi and Compare, lanes take the host's track colours as they come; the sum lane is white.

Track palette

For lanes whose host gives no track colour, which today means every Audio Unit host. Sixteen colours at three lightness levels, all at least 6:1 against the screen, none close to the orange accent or the white sum lane. They are assigned in this order, so lanes next to each other always differ clearly (ΔE 18 or more in every kind of vision), and so do lanes two apart in normal vision.

Sixteen colours can't all be told apart with colour blindness; no palette that size can. Lanes always carry the track's name, so colour is never the only way to tell them apart.

Contrast

Text needs 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2 AA). Traces are measured against the screen; they are thin lines, so they get the same bar as text.

WhereColoursRatioAA

Not measured: dividers and the screen's edge (1.3:1). They are decoration; every control is identified by its text or icon, not by its outline.

As built
