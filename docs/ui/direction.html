Sineless visual direction

The locked layout, concept A, dressed in the Orizon brand. Two treatments of the same toolbar and screen, and three places the one orange accent could go.

Frames are drawn at the default size, 1080 × 580, and scaled to fit. Colours here set the direction; step 4 tunes every value and checks contrast. Earlier steps: UI audit · layout concepts.

What both share

  • Type. Geist for every control, in sentence case at 12.5 px; Geist Mono for every number, with even-width figures. The old wide-tracked capitals are gone.
  • Colour is data. The chrome is black, white and greys. Colour appears only in the traces, so a colour always means a channel or a track.
  • Channel colours in Single: Left blue, Right pink, Mid white, Side violet. None is orange or amber, so the accent stays unique. In Multi and Compare, lanes take the host's track colours as they come; the sum is white.
  • One accent. Horizon orange marks a single thing. The frames below use it on Frozen; the last section compares the options.
  • Identity. The cashew mark and "Sineless" in Geist Semibold open the toolbar.

1 · Black

One surface

Toolbar, screen and status bar share pure black, split only by hairlines. Controls sit on a faint grey; the selected option is a raised grey, not a white block. The signal runs edge to edge, and the brighter traces are the brightest things in the window. Closest to the Orizon website's dark theme and to Apple's pro apps in full screen.

Strengths

  • The quietest chrome. Nothing competes with the traces.
  • All of the window is the screen's width; no margins.
  • Matches the brand's dark site exactly: black paper, grey surfaces.

Trade-offs

  • Selection is subtle. A raised grey is calm but easier to miss than white.
  • Edges are faint. Where the toolbar ends and the screen starts is a hairline.

2 · Graphite

A display in a case

The chrome is dark graphite and the screen is an inset black display with rounded corners, like the glass on a hardware instrument. Controls are filled grey without outlines; the selected option is white. The brand's future hardware could carry the same idea.

Strengths

  • Clear structure. Controls and screen read as two separate things at a glance.
  • Selection is unmistakable: white on graphite.
  • An object, not a page. It feels like an instrument.

Trade-offs

  • The screen loses 16 px of width to the case margins.
  • White selections draw the eye away from the traces a little more.

Where the orange goes

The brand allows one accent, for emphasis only. The same frozen Multi view with time cursors on, three ways. Host track colours can include orange; the accent stays readable because it is always a specific shape, never a lane.

On Frozen (recommended)

  • Frozen is the one state where the screen isn't live, and the audit found it too easy to miss.
  • It appears in two places, the Freeze button and the state chip, and both are text, so it never mixes with traces.

On the playhead, or on the cursors

  • Playhead: exists only in Beats, and a thin orange line sits among host colours that may be orange too.
  • Cursors: useful while measuring, but they're already labelled A and B, and a hidden cursor means no accent at all.

To decide

  • Treatment: Black or Graphite. My pick is Graphite: selection and structure read instantly, and the inset screen gives Sineless a look of its own while the traces stay the brightest thing in the window.
  • Accent: on Frozen, the playhead or the cursors. My pick is Frozen.
  • Channel colours: blue, pink, white and violet are a starting point. Step 4 checks them against each other, against the screen and for colour blindness, and builds the 16-colour fallback palette for tracks without a host colour.
