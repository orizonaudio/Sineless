Sineless UI audit

The plugin works well and measures honestly, but its interface was built feature by feature. This is where it stands today, what the new design has to carry, and the principles we start from, taken from the Orizon brand.

  1. 1
    Everything has the same weightAbout 30 controls in small uppercase, in boxes of the same size. Freeze, used constantly, looks like Hover, rarely touched.
  2. 2
    The strip spends space on structureEach group has a box and a title that restates its control. Top bar and strip take about 140 of 500 px.
  3. 3
    Controls that don't apply stay on screenIn Beats the whole Trigger group sits there dimmed; in Multi the channel toggles do too.
  4. 4
    Toggle state is ambiguous"On" is a muted channel colour, "off" is grey. L and R read as disabled when they are selected.
  5. 5
    No colour systemAmber, cyan, lilac and bone for channels; blue for active, focus and selection; green, orange and pink for states and cursors; then the host's track colours on top.
  6. 6
    Readouts are detachedPeak, RMS and Freq sit far from the traces, and it isn't clear which channel or track they measure.
  7. 7
    Lane labels are clutteredEvery lane repeats "0 dB", sharing its corner with the track name.
  8. 8
    Important state is small"Frozen" is a small chip in the corner. The sweep's playhead and dimmed previous cycle are easy to miss.
  9. 9
    Interaction is hiddenZoom, pan, cursor snapping and drag-to-change values are explained only in Help, which is off by default behind a "?".
  10. 10
    The identity is generic"SINELESS · ORIZON" in condensed caps and no mark. It doesn't feel like the Orizon brand.

Starting from scratch

This redesign covers the interface and how it's used. What the plugin does, its default values and its keyboard behaviour stay as they are. The old design's layout rules don't carry over.

What the interface has to carry

Every feature stays. Ranked by how often someone reaches for it; a first guess to confirm in the next step.

Always visible

  • The screen: traces, grid, labels, playhead, lanes
  • Display mode: Single · Multi · Compare
  • Span: Beats 1/16 beat – 16 bars or Seconds 0.5 ms – 4 s
  • Vertical scale −60 to +6 dBFS
  • Freeze and Auto-freeze, and whether it's frozen
  • Peak, RMS dBFS · Freq ≈ Hz
  • State: triggered, waiting, roll, sync, filling, no tempo

Often, one step away

  • Channel views L · R · Mid · Side, overlaid or stacked
  • Trigger mode: Auto · Normal · Free · Single, Re-arm
  • Time cursors A/B and level cursors 1/2
  • Hover readout
  • Group A–D
  • Compare: checked tracks, offset, polarity, correlation, summing gain, Preview fix

Rarely

  • Trigger source, slope, level, hysteresis, hold-off 0–1 s
  • Concert pitch A4 400–500 Hz
  • Help

What still holds

These come from the platform and the product, not from the old design.

ConstraintWhy

Principles

A draft, drawn from the brand: Apple, OpenAI and Spotify as references; minimal, confident, precise, warm.

  1. The signal is the hero. Most of the window is the screen; the chrome recedes.
  2. Colour is data. The interface is black, white and greys, so a colour always means a channel or a track. Orange is reserved for the one thing that needs attention, never for a track.
  3. Show what applies now. Controls that don't apply in the current mode are folded away, not dimmed in place.
  4. Exact, quiet numbers. Geist Mono with even figures, units in grey, readouts next to what they measure.
  5. Gestures you can find. Hints when they're useful, not only in a help mode.
  6. Calm in motion. Traces never animate; panels open with the brand's short, single easing.

Decisions

  • The ranking above holds. Auto-freeze is used a lot, so it sits with Freeze.
  • The new design sets its own minimum window size.
  • Dark only.
  • Keyboard unchanged: keys go to the host, no shortcuts of its own.
  • Defaults and behaviour unchanged, including opening in Single.
  • Which element carries the orange accent is chosen in step 3.
